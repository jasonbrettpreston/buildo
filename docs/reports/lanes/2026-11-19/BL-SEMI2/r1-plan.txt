## Verdict

This is a careful, well-instrumented plan that has learned the right lessons from the recent undeclared-field incident — the contract-site enumeration, the `red.out`/`commit.out` discipline, and the "proof no other page moved" captures are all good practice. But it is a plan that has quietly rewritten its own task: it ships the easiest half (visual mock delta) plus a meter flag, and drops the three hardest and most load-bearing deliverables (wall-distance input, owner-answer persistence, report reads the answer) without recording a split. Worse, the piece it *does* add — client-attested metering that gates navigation on a network call — replaces a server-known fact with a client-supplied parameter, sits in a direct internal contradiction with its own test (`&semi=answered` vs "refuses it with `q`"), and can lock a paying user out of their report on a transient 500 or a rate-limit hit. The review control that would have caught the scope and contract problems — the Gemini lens — is the one the plan chose not to run. Fix the four CRITICAL/HIGH items (server-side meter, scope split, the `q`+`semi` contradiction, non-gating failure path) and this is buildable; ship it as written and you get a billing integrity defect and a task marked delivered that delivered D.3–D.7 instead of D.9–D.10.
